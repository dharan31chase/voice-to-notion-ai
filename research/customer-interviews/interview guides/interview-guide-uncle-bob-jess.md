# Interview Guide: Uncle Bob (Conducted by Jess)

**Date:** Late November 2025  
**Duration:** 90-120 minutes  
**Location:** Uncle Bob & Aunt Missy's home  
**Interviewer:** Jess (niece)  
**Producer:** Dharan (equipment, notes, silent observer)  
**Recording:** Audio (Sony) + Video (DSLR) + Backup (iPhone)

---

## 🎯 Strategic Context

**Subject Profile:**
- **Name:** Uncle Bob
- **Age:** 80 years old
- **Health:** Good, can sustain 90+ minute conversation
- **Relationship:** Jess's uncle, warm family relationship
- **Branch:** Branch A (has tried memoir, stopped because hates writing)
- **Key Insight:** Waiting for someone to ask, wants to share military guilt

**Interview Goals:**

**PRIMARY (Product Research - 70%):**
- Understand conversational flow patterns and dynamics
- Observe how questions unlock stories vs create barriers
- Map tangent behavior, loop patterns, self-editing
- Test recipient-initiated model (impact of being asked by Jess)
- Validate voice authenticity for difficult emotional content

**SECONDARY (Family Preservation - 30%):**
- Capture Bob's life stories for family archive
- Preserve military service experiences and emotional processing
- Document full life arc for posterity

**Critical Hypothesis to Test:**
- Does being asked by trusted family member unlock stories he wouldn't otherwise share?
- Can voice capture emotional complexity (military guilt) that writing cannot?
- What conversational patterns does an AI need to replicate for authentic legacy capture?

**Part 1:** Life Arc - Childhood to Young Adulthood (15-20 min)

## Part 1: Life Arc - Childhood to Young Adulthood (15-20 min)

### **Q1: "Let's start at the beginning. Can you tell me about where you grew up and what your childhood was like?"**

**Interviewer Notes:**
- Let him talk freely
- Notice: Does he start chronologically or jump to specific memories?
- Your job: Listen and ask follow-ups based on what he emphasizes

**Natural Follow-up Branches:**
- If he mentions **family:** "What do you remember most about your parents?"
- If he mentions **place:** "What was [town] like back then?"
- If he mentions **hardship:** "How did that shape you?"
- If he mentions **siblings:** "What was your relationship like with them?"

---

### **Q2: "What were you like as a kid? What were you interested in?"**

**Interviewer Notes:**
- Listen for: Self-perception then vs now
- Does he describe himself or let others' descriptions come through?

---

### **Q3: "Was there a moment or experience growing up that you think really shaped who you became?"**

**Interviewer Notes:**
- Tests: Can he identify formative moments or does he see childhood as gradual development?
- If he struggles: "Or was it more gradual - just growing up in that environment?"

---

## Part 2: Military Service - The Emotional Core (30-40 min)

**[Transition when he naturally mentions military or when you sense readiness]**

### **Q4: "Can you tell me about how you ended up in the military?"**

**Interviewer Notes:**
- This opens the door - he'll likely start with joining, training, deployment
- Let him set the pace
- Notice if he goes chronological or jumps to emotionally significant moments

---

### **Q5: "What was it like when you first got there? What do you remember most?"**

**Interviewer Notes:**
- "There" = training, base, deployment - follow his language
- Listen for sensory details vs summary statements
- First memories are often emotionally loaded

---

### **Q6: [CRITICAL - THE GUILT QUESTION] "Dharan mentioned that being in the military was complicated for you. Can you tell me about that?"**

**Interviewer Notes:**
- **THIS IS THE MOMENT** - he's been waiting to talk about military guilt
- Your job: Hold space, don't fill silence, let him take his time
- He may need 10-15 seconds to gather himself - that's okay
- Don't rescue him from discomfort

**If he goes there (talks about guilt/hard experiences):**

**Follow-ups:**
- "That sounds really heavy. How have you carried that?"
- "Have you talked about this with anyone?"
- "What do you wish people understood about that experience?"
- **Silence** - just be present if he's processing

**If he doesn't go there / deflects:**
- Don't push
- Alternative: "What was the hardest part of being in the service?"
- Or: "What did you learn about yourself during that time?"
- You can circle back later if he opens up elsewhere

**Producer Note (Dharan):**
- Mark timestamp when emotion shifts
- Note body language changes
- This section may run long - let it

---

### **Q7: "What was it like coming home? How did you adjust to civilian life?"**

**Interviewer Notes:**
- Transition point - from service to reintegration
- Listen for: What was hard, what helped, who was there

---

### **Q8: "Looking back now, how do you think your military service affected the rest of your life?"**

**Interviewer Notes:**
- Tests his ability to connect past to present
- May reveal ongoing processing vs resolved experience

---

**[NATURAL BREAK POINT - After ~60 minutes]**

**Jess:** "Uncle Bob, let's take a quick break. Can I get you some water? Do you need to stretch?"

**[Stop recording during break if needed, or leave running - your call]**

## Part 4: Conversational Flow & Story Structure (15-20 min)

**[Signal the shift]**

**Jess:** "Uncle Bob, I want to change gears a bit. I'm curious about how you think about your own stories..."

---

### **Q12: "When you think about your life, what are the stories that keep coming back to you? The ones you find yourself thinking about?"**

**Interviewer Notes:**
- **GOLD MINE** - reveals his natural story architecture
- Does he list multiple stories quickly? (has mental catalog)
- Does he struggle to pick? (needs structure/prompting)
- Are they chronological or thematic?

**Producer Note (Dharan):**
- Note WHAT stories he lists first (these are his defaults)
- Note if he groups them (by time period, theme, people)
- Note if he says "I don't know" or needs time to think

---

### **Q13: "Pick one of those stories - tell me about [specific story he mentioned]."**

**Interviewer Notes:**
- You're testing story completeness - can he tell full narrative unprompted?
- Let him go as long as he wants
- **Your job: Observe, don't rescue**

**If he trails off:**
- "What happened next?"
- "Tell me more about that"

**If he self-edits ("well, I shouldn't say..."):**
- "Why do you think you remember that detail?"

**Producer Note (Dharan):**
- Track story structure: Linear? Loops back? Jumps around?
- Note where he adds detail vs summarizes
- Mark when he pauses - does he need prompting or is he thinking?

---

### **Q14: "That story - why do you think it stuck with you? What makes it important?"**

**Interviewer Notes:**
- Tests self-awareness of meaning-making
- Can he articulate significance or just report events?
- Does he connect to broader life themes?

**If he struggles:**
- "What feeling comes up when you think about it?"
- Or just: "Take your time - there's no wrong answer"

---

### **Q15: "When you've told that story before - to friends, to family - how do people usually react? What do they ask about?"**

**Interviewer Notes:**
- Reveals awareness of audience and what resonates
- Shows if he's adapted story through retelling

**Producer Note (Dharan):**
- If he says "people always ask about [X]" - note this (tells you what AI should probe)
- If he says "no one asks about X but it's important" - also note (gap between what people ask vs what matters to him)

---

### **Q16: "Are there stories you've never told anyone? Or only told a few people?"**

**Interviewer Notes:**
- Tests permission and intimacy thresholds
- May unlock new territory or explain boundaries

**If he shares something new:**
- "Thank you for trusting me with that"
- Follow naturally - don't make it A Big Deal

**If he says "I've told this before":**
- "Who have you told?" (verify audience - just Missy? or broader?)

**Producer Note (Dharan):**
- Does voice + Jess's presence unlock stories?
- Or does he say "there's nothing I haven't told"?

---

### **Q17: "If you were teaching someone how to interview you - like if I was going to do this again with you next month - what should I ask? What should I make sure to cover?"**

**Interviewer Notes:**
- **PURE GOLD FOR PRODUCT** - he's designing your interview AI
- Does he have clear structure? ("ask about X, Y, Z in that order")
- Focus on chronology or themes?
- Specific people, places, periods?

**Follow-up if needed:**
- "Should I ask about feelings or just facts?"
- "Are there topics I should avoid?"
- "What's the most important thing to capture?"

**Producer Note (Dharan):**
- THIS TELLS YOU WHAT YOUR AI SHOULD DO
- Note exact language - his words are your product spec

---

### **Q18: "You mentioned you've been waiting for someone to ask. What made today feel like the right time? And what made me the right person to ask?"**

**Interviewer Notes:**
- Tests recipient-initiated model dynamics
- Role of relationship vs role of "being asked"

**Listen for:**
- "You're family" (relationship-dependent)
- "You asked" (action-dependent)
- "I trust you" (safety-dependent)
- "I'm ready now" (timing-dependent)

**Producer Note (Dharan):**
- Critical for product: Can AI replicate this or does it require human?
- If it's about Jess specifically → need human facilitation
- If it's about being asked → AI can unlock this

## 📋 Interview Principles for Jess

### **The "Inheriting" Method:**

1. **You're not a journalist extracting information**
   - You're his niece who wants to know him
   - Curiosity, not interrogation
   - Connection, not content capture

2. **Silence is your friend**
   - After asking question, stop talking
   - Count to 10 in your head if needed
   - Best stuff comes after he processes

3. **Follow the energy**
   - When voice changes → lean in
   - When he lights up → go deeper
   - When he gets heavy → hold space
   - When he deflects → respect it (can circle back)

4. **It's okay to cry**
   - If you get emotional, that's beautiful
   - If he gets emotional, just be present
   - "Take your time" / "That's a lot"
   - Offer breaks if needed

5. **You can go off-script**
   - These questions are scaffolding, not requirements
   - If he takes you somewhere unexpected, follow
   - Goal is HIS story, not your questions

### **Trauma-Informed Boundaries:**

**If he shows distress:**
- "We can take a break"
- "We don't have to talk about this if you don't want to"
- "What would feel better to talk about?"

**If he dissociates/shuts down:**
- Don't push
- Gentle pivot: "Let me ask you about something else..."
- Bring him back to present: "What you're sharing is really important"

**The Missy Question:**

If he says "Missy knows this better" or "Maybe we should have Missy here":
- **Your call:** Bring her in (she's there) OR gently redirect: "I want to hear your version first, then maybe we can talk to her together later"
